# VTA-NAcc resting-state coupling is stable across the lifespan

Analysis code and derived data for the manuscript:

> **Hu, X.** *Resting-State Functional Coupling Between the Ventral Tegmental
> Area and Nucleus Accumbens Is Stable Across the Lifespan: A Replication in
> the NKI-Rockland Sample.*

This repository reproduces every statistic in the paper. It contains **no
imaging data**; the original scans remain at their public source (see below).

---

## What the study does

[Murty et al. (2018)](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.3508-17.2018) reported
that functional coupling between the ventral tegmental area (VTA) and the
nucleus accumbens (NAcc) varies with age in a **motivational** context
(p = 0.0004) but shows **no age effect at rest** (p = 0.56), in a longitudinal
sample of 180 participants aged 10-30.

This study replicates the resting-state null in the **NKI-Rockland** sample:
307 participants, age 9-83, TR = 1400 ms. The age range covers roughly four
times the span of the original study and extends into middle and old age.

**Headline result:** no age effect on resting-state VTA-NAcc coupling
(linear r = -0.042, p = 0.46, n = 307), and the null survives head-motion
control (see below).

## Method summary

**Regions of interest** (spheres in MNI space, r = 4 mm; coordinates follow
Murty et al., 2014):

| region | x | y | z |
|---|---|---|---|
| VTA | 0 | -16 | -14 |
| NAcc L | -10 | 12 | -8 |
| NAcc R | 10 | 12 | -8 |

**Coupling.** Mean time series per sphere; detrend, z-standardise, band-pass
0.01-0.08 Hz; Pearson r between VTA and each NAcc hemisphere, averaged; Fisher
z-transform. Scans with fewer than 30 volumes are dropped.

**Statistics.** Three models: simple linear correlation, inverse-age (1/age,
replicating Murty et al.), and multiple regression with sex. The primary
analysis uses **one scan per participant** (first scan, sorted by session) to
avoid pseudoreplication; a full-scan analysis is reported for completeness.

## Head motion

This is the analysis the reviewers of a null fMRI result will care about most,
so it is reported in full.

**Where the FD values come from.** The raw BIDS release has no motion
parameters, and the C-PAC derivatives were reported in the manuscript as
covering only the TR = 645 ms acquisition. That is not the case: C-PAC also
provides FD for the **acq-1400** resting scan, at

```
Outputs/C-PAC/<sub>/output/pipeline_analysis_nuisance/
    <sub>_<ses>/frame_wise_displacement_power/_scan_rest_acq-1400/FD.1D
```

`03_fetch_fd.py` retrieves these. Coverage: FD was retrievable for 307
subjects, of which **250** had FD for their primary scan. C-PAC did not process
every session (notably, some sessions are absent entirely), so the remaining
57 participants are retained in the primary analysis but excluded from the
motion-controlled models.

**Why the null is not a motion artefact.**

1. Motion *increases* with age (r = +0.18, p = 0.005), and motion is *not*
   related to coupling (r = +0.06, p = 0.38). The usual mechanism by which
   motion produces a spurious age effect therefore does not operate here.
2. Adding mean FD as a covariate leaves the age effect non-significant
   (p = 0.82 for the linear model; p = 0.64 for the inverse-age model).
3. Excluding high-motion participants at five thresholds (mean FD <= 1.0, 0.7,
   0.5, 0.3, 0.2 mm) leaves the age effect non-significant throughout
   (all p > 0.32).
4. The full-scan analysis (304 scans, 269 participants) with FD as a covariate
   is likewise non-significant (p = 0.75).
5. Participants with and without FD do not differ reliably in age
   (40.7 vs 45.2 years, Welch p = 0.15).

**Remaining limitation.** FD is modelled as a covariate and used for
exclusion; the coupling estimates themselves were computed from the raw BIDS
images without nuisance regression or volume scrubbing. A fully preprocessed
recomputation (e.g. from C-PAC or fMRIPrep derivatives with confound
regression) would be the strongest version of this analysis and is left to
future work.

## A note on the statistics module

`code/_stats.py` implements the Student-t distribution through the regularised
incomplete beta function, because the analysis environment had no SciPy. It is
validated against reference values in its `__main__` block:

```
t = 2.0,  df = 10   -> two-sided p = 0.073388
t = 1.96, df = 1e6  -> two-sided p = 0.049996
```

`00_quick_reproduce.py` also reproduces the published p-values exactly.

## Robustness analyses (Sections 3.8 and 3.9)

`code/05_fetch_preprocessed.py` and `code/06_preproc_sensitivity.py` repeat the
coupling computation on the **C-PAC preprocessed derivatives** of the same
acquisition (motion-corrected, nuisance-regressed) and under twelve alternative
ROI definitions.

**Download requirement.** Roughly 142 MB per scan. C-PAC did not process every
session, so of the 409 scans in the raw plan, 114 returned HTTP 404 and 190
were retrieved, covering 171 participants with a usable motion estimate. The
script is resumable: it skips `(subject, session)` pairs already present in
`data_derived/coupling_preprocessed.csv`.

> **Completion.** All 409 planned scans were processed: 220 yielded coupling
> values (200 participants) and 171 returned HTTP 404 because C-PAC did not
> process those sessions. Applying the same scan-selection rule as the primary
> analysis gives n = 178 participants available under both pipelines.

**Outputs**

| file | contents |
|---|---|
| `data_derived/coupling_preprocessed.csv` | per-scan coupling under 12 ROI definitions |
| `results/preproc_analysis.txt` | main result, pipeline agreement, ROI sensitivity |
| `results/equiv_and_lmm.txt` | TOST equivalence bounds and Bayes factor; random-intercept mixed model |
| `results/forest_values.csv` | the values plotted in Figure 3 |
| `results/Fig1`–`Fig4` | manuscript figures |

**Key outputs of these analyses**

- Preprocessed data, n = 171: age effect non-significant (r = +0.090, p = 0.244),
  point estimate opposite in sign to the raw-BIDS analysis (r = −0.042).
- Agreement between the two pipelines: r = 0.284 (p = 0.0002, n = 171).
- Test–retest reliability, 40 BAS1–BAS2 pairs: r = 0.279, ICC(1,1) = 0.461.
- No ROI variant gave a significant zero-order age correlation (all p > 0.10).

`results/reliability.json` records every test-retest estimate. They differ by

## Equivalence testing and Bayes factor (Section 3.6)

The primary age-coupling correlation is r = -0.042 (n = 307), 90% CI
[-0.136, +0.052]. Two one-sided tests (TOST) on the Fisher-z scale give a
smallest supported equivalence bound of **r = 0.136**: age effects of
|rho| >= 0.14 are excluded, smaller effects are not.

The Bayes factor for H0: rho = 0 against a two-sided alternative uses the
JZS default prior for correlations (Rouder et al., 2009; Ly et al., 2016):

| Cauchy prior scale | BF01 | BF10 |
|---|---|---|
| 0.5 | **8.49** | 0.118 |
| 1.0 | **16.74** | 0.060 |

By Jeffreys' conventions this is moderate-to-strong evidence for the absence
of an age effect. Reproduce with `code/07_equivalence_lmm.py`.

| subset | participants | pairs | r | ICC(1,1) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| BAS1-BAS2 only (same session type) | 40 | 40 | +0.279 | +0.262 |
| all session pairs | 55 | 182 | +0.111 | +0.118 |
| motion-matched subset (mixed model) | 22 | 57 scans | - | -0.042 |
| C-PAC preprocessed, all | 13 | 33 scans | - | -0.095 |

The gap between the same-type and all-pairs figures reflects genuine
heterogeneity across session types (FLU sessions occur during an influenza
challenge, TRT sessions during treatment), not estimation error. Because the
primary analysis draws its single scan almost entirely from baseline sessions,
ICC ~ 0.26 is the more relevant figure, and 0.12 is carried forward as a
conservative lower bound.

**Why this matters for the manuscript.** Unreliable measurement attenuates
observed correlations. Correcting the equivalence bound of r = 0.136 for
attenuation gives a true bound of roughly 0.27-0.40, so the study excludes
large true age effects and provides only weak evidence against a medium one.
This is stated in Sections 4.6 and 4.7 of the manuscript.

## Reference verification (v7)

All bibliographic records were checked against Crossref and Europe PMC. Three
errors present in earlier drafts were corrected:

1. **Morales et al. (2024)** has **18 authors**, not six. The earlier list named
   S. M. Carlson (correct surname, wrong initial), included C. L. Ehlers who is
   not an author, and omitted thirteen others.
2. **Peterson et al. (2017)** is titled *"The effects of age, from young to
   middle adulthood, and gender on resting state functional connectivity of the
   dopaminergic midbrain"* — the earlier title omitted both the age range and
   the gender component.
3. **Murty et al. (2014)** is *NeuroImage, 100*, **580–589** with DOI
   `10.1016/j.neuroimage.2014.06.047`. An earlier draft would have carried
   `...2014.06.043`, which resolves to an unrelated paper by Durrleman et al.

DOIs were added for all ten journal references. `results/reference_check.txt`
contains the raw Crossref/Europe PMC output used for the verification; the
scripts to reproduce it are not shipped because the check is a one-off.

Session-level details are unaffected: the derived data files, the analysis
scripts and `results/quick_reproduce.txt` all correspond to the current
manuscript.
